Cha Portal should treat VRR as a scheduling fix it can make now on the node and HDR as a colour pipeline it builds in two halves, measuring before it goes past the first step of either. The largest timing loss is in Steam sessions on the node: a game frame waits for nested gamescope's `-r` timer and then for the compositor's encode tick, two fixed clocks of the same rate that add up to two frame intervals of pure waiting (about 16.7 ms on average at 60 fps by the researchers' arithmetic, unmeasured) and quantise a game that runs below the cap twice. Compositing when gamescope commits, under a Sunshine-style budget, sending gamescope a zero refresh so that its `--adaptive-sync` mode paints when the game does, and keeping each encoder at the cap's rate with its one-frame VBV removes both waits without touching a client. Desktop sessions should keep the 240 Hz feedback clock, because Chrome paces itself from presentation feedback and drops to 60 fps when the refresh it is given is zero. On the client side, Chrome never runs a ProMotion panel at the stream's rate: 90 fps judders there and a 60 fps stream may halve its compositor rate, so only the native player in native full screen can show each frame when it arrives, and C2.4's `CAMetalDisplayLink` work has to beat today's immediate present on a glass-to-glass rig before it ships. HDR's cheap half is 10-bit SDR, which exercises every layer from encoder to shader with no colour management (NVENC converts 8 to 10 bits in hardware for AV1, PyroWave has R16 planes, VideoToolbox needs one format request), plus a canned HDR10 clip played through each client path (WebCodecs into `<video>`, PyroWave into an `rgba16float` canvas, an extended-linear `Rgba16Float` wgpu 30 surface) to learn which can light the XDR panel before the node changes at all. The expensive half is on the node: a complete `wp_color_manager_v1` server (MIT code exists in a Smithay fork), per-surface decoding into a linear FP16 composite, one compute pass to BT.2020/PQ P010 that NVENC, VA-API and PyroWave share, HDR10 static metadata on every IDR, and gamescope rebuilt from upstream 3.17.0 with `--hdr-enabled` and `DXVK_HDR=1`. Desktops should stay SDR unless an HDR surface is on screen and the client display is HDR, SDR clients should get SDR made on the node, and Cha should reject HDR over WebRTC, HLG and dynamic metadata, 8-bit PQ, NVENC's built-in RGB→YUV conversion for HDR, 10-bit H.264 and CPU-node HDR, and PQ passthrough as the native player's default. Nobody has published a measurement of VRR against fixed refresh inside a streaming client, of HDR's latency in any browser, or of a PQ frame reaching EDR through a track generator, so Cha has to produce those numbers itself, starting with the glass-to-glass rig C2.4 deferred and a timestamp of how long gamescope's frames wait.

*Evidence tags used throughout:* **[V]** vendor claim or vendor documentation; **[D]** single developer, single tester or PR author; **[R]** research paper measuring its own method; **[I]** independent measurement by a press lab or third-party study; **[E]** the researchers' own estimate or arithmetic, measured by nobody; **[C]** Cha Portal's own measurement or a fact read from Cha's code. Behaviour read directly from another project's source at a named commit carries no tag: it says what that code does on that date, not how well it works.

## Two fixed clocks on the node cost a Steam session up to two frames

HDR and VRR meet at frame timing and at the presenter, but the first place timing goes wrong is upstream of both, inside the node.

### A game frame waits twice before it is encoded

`tick()` in [`crates/cha-streamer/src/compositor/mod.rs`](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/compositor/mod.rs) runs two fixed clocks [C]. A timerfd tick, 240 Hz by default and rounded by `tick_rate` up to a multiple of the encode rate (270 Hz for 90 fps), sends every surface its frame callbacks and its presentation feedback with `Refresh::Fixed(tick_period)` and `Kind::Vsync`. An encode tick at `--fps` (60, 90 or 120) composites only if something is damaged. The compositor advertises no fifo, commit-timing, tearing-control or colour-management protocol, and Smithay is pinned at git rev `118e34ff` [C]. At that pin Smithay already has fifo and commit-timing modules that place commit blockers and leave signalling to the compositor, and a version-2 presentation global whose `Refresh::Variable(min)` sends a zero refresh to clients that bind version 1 ([Smithay fifo @118e34ff](https://github.com/Smithay/smithay/blob/118e34ffc9b99854a2230c4805e1f37dc9029edb/src/wayland/fifo/mod.rs); [presentation @118e34ff](https://github.com/Smithay/smithay/blob/118e34ffc9b99854a2230c4805e1f37dc9029edb/src/wayland/presentation/mod.rs)).

Nested gamescope ignores all of the callbacks. Its Wayland backend binds `wp_presentation` at **version 1**, requests no `wl_surface.frame` at all, runs its own vblank timer at `-r` re-armed from each host presentation timestamp, adopts any non-zero host refresh as its output rate, and treats the host as VRR only when the refresh it receives is zero; `IsVRRActive()` is `cv_adaptive_sync && m_bHostCompositorIsCurrentlyVRR` ([WaylandBackend.cpp @89b9e6ee](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/89b9e6ee61513767f4771e0022b669a48efb5780/src/Backends/WaylandBackend.cpp); [vblankmanager.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/89b9e6ee61513767f4771e0022b669a48efb5780/src/vblankmanager.cpp)). [`images/steam/steam-gaming`](../../../images/steam/steam-gaming) launches `gamescope --backend wayland -W w -H h -w w -h h -r "$CHA_REFRESH" -e --mangoapp`, with no `--adaptive-sync` and no `--hdr-enabled` [C]. Under Cha, then, gamescope logs a 240 Hz output, publishes 240 in `GAMESCOPE_DISPLAY_REFRESH_RATE_FEEDBACK`, keeps VRR off and composites at `-r` phase-locked to whichever 240 Hz tick "presented" its last commit, and the 240 Hz callbacks that spike S2 found halve Chrome's click-to-screen latency [C] do nothing for it.

A game frame therefore waits for gamescope's next `-r` wake, and gamescope's composite then waits for Cha's next encode tick, although both clocks run at the same rate.

| Stream rate (`-r` = `--fps`) | Game present → gamescope wake | gamescope commit → Cha encode tick | Both waits, mean / worst [E] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 0–16.7 ms, mean 8.3 | a fixed offset of 0–16.7 ms on the 240 Hz grid | ≈16.7 / 33.3 ms |
| 90 | 0–11.1 ms, mean 5.6 | 0–11.1 ms | ≈11.1 / 22.2 ms |
| 120 | 0–8.3 ms, mean 4.2 | 0–8.3 ms | ≈8.3 / 16.7 ms |

The means assume the second wait is spread uniformly; in fact its phase is fixed for a session somewhere on the 240 Hz grid, and nobody has measured where it falls, which is why logging gamescope's commit time against Cha's composite time is the first measurement on the node. The only streaming-specific study of the mechanism agrees with the arithmetic: in NSDI 2025's instrumented cloud-gaming loop each uncoordinated VSync stage added a delay "nearly uniform[ly] distribut[ed] between 0 and 16.7 ms", and five such stages made up **35.7% of non-network latency** (mean 43.9 ms) and most of its jitter [I] ([NSDI '25](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi25-li-yang.pdf)). Against Cha's ~5 ms median LAN send→shown [C], even the 120 fps case costs more than the whole transport path, and a game below `-r` is quantised twice: gamescope repeats frames at `-r`, then the encode tick repeats or drops them again [E].

### Composite on gamescope's commit, and keep the 240 Hz tick for Chrome

Four changes put Steam sessions on the game's cadence. Run gamescope with `--adaptive-sync`, which works nested ([gamescope #1694](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/issues/1694)), or have Cha's X helpers set the `GAMESCOPE_VRR_ENABLED` root atom. Send gamescope's surface a zero refresh, which in Smithay is either `Refresh::Variable(1/cap)` or `Unknown`, since a version-1 binder receives zero either way. Send `presented` at the moment Cha actually composites gamescope's buffer, because gamescope anchors `IsVRRFlipReady` to that timestamp, and `discarded` for a buffer replaced before it was composited. And set `-r` to the stream's ceiling. In VRR mode gamescope then paints when the game has a new frame, at most once per `-r` interval; mangoapp and cursor repaints do not drive it; and Steam's per-game FPS limit becomes a time schedule instead of an integer divisor of the refresh, so 45 or 50 fps limits work ([steamcompmgr.cpp @89b9e6ee](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/89b9e6ee61513767f4771e0022b669a48efb5780/src/steamcompmgr.cpp)). On Cha's side the encode tick gives way to a composite on the driving surface's commit, no sooner than one cap interval after the previous composite, using Sunshine's late-frame rule `next = max(next, t − T/4) + T` ([Sunshine wlgrab.cpp](https://github.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/blob/master/src/platform/linux/wlgrab.cpp)) or pixelflux's pull-forward budget ([pixelflux pace.rs](https://github.com/linuxserver/pixelflux/blob/HEAD/src/pace.rs)). The open-source hosts have converged on exactly this: Sunshine captures on commit, Selkies 2.2 cut an application's redraw-to-encoder time from **11 ms to 2.6 ms** at the median by publishing changes at once instead of at the next tick [D] ([Selkies 2.2.0](https://newreleases.io/project/github/selkies-project/selkies/release/2.2.0)), Vibepollo asks Windows for a 1000 Hz virtual display so frames are captured as drawn ([Vibepollo configuration](https://github.com/Nonary/Vibepollo/blob/HEAD/docs/configuration.md)), and GeForce NOW "var[ies] the stream rate to the client" [V] ([NVIDIA](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/CES-2024-geforce-now-activision-blizzard-day-passes-g-sync)). KWin's own VRR loop has the same shape: present no sooner than one vblank after the last frame, and let only the active window drive the refresh while other surfaces wait up to 1/30 s ([KWin renderloop.cpp](https://invent.kde.org/plasma/kwin/-/blob/master/src/core/renderloop.cpp)).

The traps are known. Sunshine measured that removing its paced copies on a 60 Hz headless output dropped delivery from 60 to about 55 fps, because the paced copies were what kept self-pacing clients on time [D] ([wlgrab.cpp](https://github.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/blob/master/src/platform/linux/wlgrab.cpp)), so feedback must keep flowing whether or not a composite happens. gamescope's `adaptive_sync_idle_hold` keeps the Steam UI at the refresh rate with repeated frames, which Cha has to recognise (from commit damage, if repeats carry none, or a cheap hash of a downscaled frame) and not encode. A stream that goes silent needs a keep-alive: Sunshine re-encodes the last image at half the stream rate by default because "many clients expect a constant stream of video frames" ([Sunshine configuration.md](https://github.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/blob/master/docs/configuration.md)), and a VRR display below its floor repeats frames itself through low-framerate compensation [V] ([AMD FreeSync](https://amd.com/en/products/graphics/technologies/freesync.html)). Uncapped games need Steam's limit, or `GAMESCOPE_FPS_LIMIT` written by Cha, with `-r` bounding what reaches the compositor. The version matters too. Cha builds Ubuntu 26.04's gamescope with one patch, [`gamescope-wayland-mangoapp.patch`](../../../images/steam/gamescope-wayland-mangoapp.patch), whose hunk position shows a source older than 3.16.31; upstream 3.17.0 (2026-10-23) reworked the schedule so that "an explicit rate owns its cadence" and the VRR branch "can follow a newly ready frame" ([VBlankSchedule.h @3.17.0](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/3.17.0/src/Utils/VBlankSchedule.h)), and the same release carries the HDR fixes in the node section below, so rebuilding from 3.17.0 with the mangoapp patch is one change that serves both threads. With `-r` at the maximum and VRR on, `-r` becomes only an upper bound, which suggests Cha's live 60/90/120 switch could finally reach gamescope apps; the [streamer README](../../../crates/cha-streamer/README.md) says today that "a gamescope app (Steam) can't change its refresh while running" [C]. That needs a spike.

Desktop sessions differ, and the evidence says so plainly. The compositor's own comment records that "Chrome follows presentation feedback, not frame callbacks" [C], and S2 found a 240 Hz compositor cut click→browser-compositor from 77–83 ms to 34–35 ms, while Chrome "drew at 60 fps even with 240 Hz callbacks" when feedback came at 60 Hz [C] ([PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md)). Chromium under a zero refresh "fails to VSync unless the hardware cursor is moved" ([KDE bug 478938](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=478938)), and a KWin merge request says zero "breaks applications that rely on a non-zero refresh … (e.g. chromium)" ([KWin MR !5697](https://invent.kde.org/plasma/kwin/-/merge_requests/5697)). Desktop sessions should keep `Fixed(1/240)`; compositing on Chrome's commit could still remove up to one encode interval there, as a latency change rather than a VRR one.

| Session | Feedback sent | Composite trigger | gamescope flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steam (gamescope) | refresh 0 (`Variable`/`Unknown` to a v1 binder); `presented` at the real composite; `discarded` for replaced buffers | gamescope's commit, ≥ 1/cap after the last, Sunshine's late-frame rule; repeats skipped; keep-alive at a low rate | `--adaptive-sync`, `-r` = cap, 3.17.0 |
| Desktop (Chrome, KDE, XFCE) | `Fixed(1/240)` on the 240 Hz tick, as today | damage at the encode tick today; on commit later, as a latency change | n/a |

fifo-v1 and commit-timing-v1 matter only for native Wayland clients on Cha's own socket. Mesa's FIFO mode without fifo-v1 falls back to frame callbacks ([Mesa wsi_common_wayland.c](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/blob/d3c007c5dcef02dcaf0745754f21a879601047ff/src/vulkan/wsi/wsi_common_wayland.c)), so a Vulkan app run directly on Cha renders at up to 240 fps and three frames in four are dropped at a 60 fps encode [E], and SDL3 prefers Wayland over X11 only when both protocols are present ([SDL #9345](https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL/pull/9345)). Proton games in Steam sessions are X11 clients of gamescope, so Cha's protocol choices reach them only through gamescope. Cha should expose fifo-v1 when a desktop client needs it, signalling barriers when a composite consumes the surface plus a fallback timer, and hold commit-timing back until it is wired to the composite scheduler: cosmic-comp's RFC implementation reports abnormal behaviour with NVIDIA's Vulkan driver [D] ([cosmic-comp #2797](https://github.com/pop-os/cosmic-comp/pull/2797)). Presentation-time v3, whose `fixed_rate` and `variable_rate` flags (wayland-protocols 1.50, 2026-10-29) exist to feed `VK_EXT_present_timing`, is not yet in Smithay and is a watch item ([wayland-protocols b9edf66](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/wayland/wayland-protocols/-/commit/b9edf66597586c914b5b1ff3d964d7ed201adab9)).

### The encoders keep the cap's rate and drop one false flag

No encoder Cha uses documents variable-frame-rate behaviour under low-latency CBR. NVENC's guide recommends CBR with "a single frame = bitrate/framerate" VBV and treats `inputTimeStamp` as a unique frame ID for reference invalidation, and says nothing about irregular input [V] ([NVENC guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/video-codec-sdk/13.0/nvenc-video-encoder-api-prog-guide/index.html)). Sunshine configures NVENC exactly as Cha does — a fixed `frameRateNum`, a one-frame VBV and the frame index as timestamp — and already encodes at an irregular cadence whenever capture is event-driven ([Sunshine nvenc_base.cpp](https://github.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/blob/master/src/nvenc/nvenc_base.cpp)). Cha should keep `frameRateNum` and the VBV at the cap: each frame still gets about one cap-frame of bits, the bit rate falls in proportion to the game's frame rate, and latency never rises [E, unverified against NVENC's internals]. The 8-frame RFI history in [`crates/cha-nvenc/src/nvenc.rs`](../../../crates/cha-nvenc/src/nvenc.rs) is fixed in frames, so at a low game rate it reaches further back in time, and Cha already counts losses by index [C]. One old forum report says a frame-rate reconfigure forces an IDR; Cha measured a 60→120 switch in place on the RTX 4090 over 480 frames with no keyframe [C] ([streamer README](../../../crates/cha-streamer/README.md)), and Cha's own measurement wins for its SDK and driver. One statement in the bitstream becomes false: Cha's H.264 SPS writer for VA-API sets `fixed_frame_rate_flag` with a `time_scale` of twice the rate ([`bitstream.rs` line 361](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/encoder/bitstream.rs)) [C], which should be cleared or written for the cap with the flag at 0. The AV1 sequence header writes no timing info ([`bitstream/av1.rs` line 133](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/encoder/bitstream/av1.rs)) [C], and PyroWave's budget is per frame ([`pyro.rs`](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/pyro.rs)) [C], so neither needs a change. How VA-API drivers' HRD and x264 and SVT-AV1 behave with long gaps was not researched; a search snippet of an old Sunshine report of AMD's encoder freezing on long gaps is unverified.

## Only the native player in full screen can show the game's cadence

### Chrome on ProMotion latches at 120, 60 or 30 Hz

Chromium drives a `CADisplayLink` on macOS 14 and later but never asks ProMotion for an arbitrary rate: `hw_takes_any_refresh_rate_` is only ever assigned `false`, so lower rates are emulated by skipping vsyncs in powers of two ([external_begin_frame_source_mac.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/components/viz/service/frame_sinks/external_begin_frame_source_mac.cc)). When only video is updating, viz's `OnlyVideoMatcher` picks the longest supported interval that has a "simple cadence" with the video ([frame_interval_matchers.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/components/viz/service/display/frame_interval_matchers.cc)).

| Stream rate | What Chrome on a 120 Hz ProMotion panel does [E, from source] | Effect on Cha |
|---|---|---|
| 120 fps | stays at 120 Hz | 1:1, no judder |
| 60 fps, page otherwise idle | may pick 16.7 ms and latch every second vsync | mean latch wait rises from ≈4.2 to ≈8.3 ms |
| 90 fps | no simple cadence, so it stays at 120 Hz | holds of 8.3, 8.3 and 16.7 ms: a 30 Hz judder |

Whether `OnlyVideoMatcher` engages for MediaStream video at all is unknown, because the researchers could not read `webmediaplayer_ms_compositor.cc`, and any rAF loop or CSS animation on the page keeps the compositor at 120 Hz. PLAN's own browser baseline already budgets that "the 60 fps cadence on a 120 Hz panel adds up to one stream frame of wait" [C] ([PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) §3.6). Safari still defaults to "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps", which would cap a canvas path such as PyroWave's at about 60 Hz unless the user turns it off ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/enable-smoother-120hz-browsing-in-safari/)); nothing in Chromium on Windows requests VRR or tearing for video or canvas; and no one has measured Firefox. Chrome's choice is visible in Perfetto as `ExternalBeginFrameSourceMac::SetPreferredInterval` with its `vsync_subsampling_factor`, so the policy can be checked cheaply: on Chrome with a ProMotion panel, offer 60 and 120 and keep 90 for clients whose display is VRR or runs at a multiple of 90.

This also bounds what node-side VRR buys a browser. A variable-rate stream arriving at a 120 Hz latch is quantised in 8.3 ms steps instead of the 16.7 ms steps of today's 60 fps stream, so a game below the cap looks steadier in Chrome too as long as Chrome stays at 120 Hz [E]; but the panel never follows the game, and the latency gain is the node's two removed waits, not the client's.

### The native player presents immediately today and must earn its pacing

[`crates/cha-player/src/render/mod.rs`](../../../crates/cha-player/src/render/mod.rs) prefers Mailbox, then Immediate, then Fifo, with `desired_maximum_frame_latency: 1` [C]; Metal offers only Fifo and Immediate, so the player runs Immediate with two drawables, presenting each frame as it decodes ([wgpu-hal metal/adapter.rs](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs)). Apple recommends `CAMetalDisplayLink` for pacing, which hands the app a drawable and a target presentation time [V] ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametaldisplaylink.md); [DTS on the forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/763426)). One developer's measurement found its update-to-target-present latency at **41.6 ms (5 refreshes) windowed and 16.3 ms (2 refreshes) in full screen** at 120 Hz, with actual presents within 0.01 ms of target and `preferredFrameLatency` 1 or 2 making no difference [D] ([frameclock PR #9](https://github.com/forest-rs/frameclock/pull/9)). Adaptive-Sync needs an Apple-silicon Mac, an Adaptive-Sync display and a full-screen app, and then a frame "is presented to the display immediately after it is done, so your app incurs only a 1ms penalty", where a fixed display shows a late frame for 16 ms instead of 8 [V] ([WWDC21 10147](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10147/)). moonlight-qt uses the display link only with V-Sync on, with `preferredFrameRateRange` fixed at the stream's rate, newest frame wins, waiting at most half an interval for a new one ([vt_metal.mm](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt/blob/master/app/streaming/video/ffmpeg-renderers/vt_metal.mm)), and its users found VRR on macOS worked only in "Fullscreen" mode with V-Sync on [D] ([moonlight-qt #1509](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt/issues/1509)). Apple has not said whether the built-in ProMotion panel follows a windowed app's frames. wgpu has no display-link or timed-present API, so the paced path has to bypass `wgpu::Surface` for presentation: wrap `update.drawable.texture` with `create_texture_from_hal`, draw with wgpu, and present on wgpu's own Metal queue so ordering comes for free; no Rust project doing exactly this was found.

VRR shows network and decode jitter directly, so presenting on arrival needs a small hold. Nonary's VRR fork of moonlight-qt offers budgets of half a frame (≈4.17 ms at 120 fps), one frame, and 24 ms [D] ([vrr18](https://newreleases.io/project/github/Nonary/moonlight-qt/release/v6.1.0-vrr18)); NSDI's LoopTailor cut the client VSync wait from 8 ms to 3 ms by aligning it with the remote side [R], while simply disabling VSync matched that latency but brought "unstable frame rate and screen tearing" [I] ([NSDI '25](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi25-li-yang.pdf)). The streaming failure reports are about uneven arrival, such as a FreeSync TV swinging between 50 and 120 Hz on a steady 89–90 fps stream [D] ([moonlight-qt #1545](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt/issues/1545)). C2.4 should therefore compare three modes, windowed and in native full screen, on the same rig: today's Immediate; display-link pacing at (fps, fps, fps) with Moonlight's half-interval wait; and present-on-arrival in full-screen VRR with a hold of at most half a frame. Decode-complete time and the drawable's presented time come first, since wgpu exposes neither. Given frameclock's three-refresh windowed penalty [D], Cha's ≤12 ms p50 native target ([PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) §3.6) is probably reachable only in native full screen [E]. On a later Windows player, VRR needs a flip-model swap chain, `ALLOW_TEARING` and sync interval 0 [V] ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3ddxgi/variable-refresh-rate-displays)); on Linux it is a compositor setting plus FIFO or IMMEDIATE presentation, with `VK_EXT_present_timing` reachable only through hooks on wgpu's unreleased trunk ([wgpu CHANGELOG](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/CHANGELOG.md)).

## Ten-bit SDR builds half of HDR before any colour management

### More bits cost little and touch every layer HDR needs

The evidence for 10-bit SDR is old and from VOD encoders, but it points one way. A VCIP paper reports Main10 coding of 8-bit content saving "around 5% BD-rate … in luma" over Main, per a search summary of its text [I] ([Vanne et al.](https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/10024/128707/1/vanne_vcip_v115.pdf)); a 2026 longitudinal NVENC study attributes part of newer HEVC encoders' gains to native 10-bit internal processing even with 8-bit input [I] ([arXiv 2605.01187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01187)); Apple's own SDR HEVC ladder uses Main10 [V] ([Apple HLS spec](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/http-live-streaming/hls-authoring-specification-for-apple-devices)); and GeForce NOW gives its 10-bit "Color precision" mode to Edge and Chrome on Windows, Chromebooks and Samsung and LG TVs because it "enhances visual quality even if you do not have an HDR display" [V] ([GFN release highlights](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce-now/release-highlights/)). No one has measured low-delay game or desktop content. On the encoder side it is nearly free where it matters: NVENC's AV1 config says the hardware does an 8→10-bit conversion internally, while for HEVC "prior to Blackwell, NVENC performs an internal CUDA 8 to 10-bit conversion" [V] ([nvEncodeAPI.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFmpeg/nv-codec-headers/master/include/ffnvcodec/nvEncodeAPI.h); [NVENC guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/video-codec-sdk/13.0/nvenc-video-encoder-api-prog-guide/index.html)); VA-API encodes from P010; PyroWave has R16 planes.

What makes 10-bit SDR the right first step is that it changes every layer HDR later needs except colour management. Cha's VA-API path offers only HEVC Main 8-bit and AV1 profile 0 8-bit [C], and its own header writers need `general_profile_idc = 2` with `bit_depth_*_minus8 = 2` for HEVC and `high_bitdepth = 1` for AV1 (from the specs [E]). The browser player's WebCodecs strings are all 8-bit (`hev1.1.6.L153.B0`, `avc1.640033`, `av01.0.13M.08`, [`player.ts` line 60](../../../web/packages/player/src/player.ts)) [C]; the 10-bit forms are `hev1.2.4.L153.B0` and `av01.0.13M.10`, which `VideoDecoder.isConfigSupported` should confirm on each target ([AV1 ISOBMFF](https://aomediacodec.github.io/av1-isobmff/); [StaZhu](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StaZhu/enable-chromium-hevc-hardware-decoding/main/README.md)). The native player's VideoToolbox decoder requests only `kCVPixelFormatType_420YpCbCr8BiPlanarVideoRange` and `…8BiPlanarFullRange` ([`videotoolbox.rs` lines 216–219](../../../crates/cha-player/src/video/videotoolbox.rs)) [C], so a 10-bit stream reaches the shader as 8 bits, even though [`render/color.rs`](../../../crates/cha-player/src/render/color.rs) already handles 10-bit samples and the BT.2020 matrix and [`present/metal.rs`](../../../crates/cha-player/src/present/metal.rs) already maps 16-bit planes to `R16Unorm`/`Rg16Unorm` [C]; requesting the `420YpCbCr10BiPlanar` formats is the whole change. WebRTC is excluded from the start: libwebrtc's dav1d wrapper returns an error for any picture that is not 8 bits ([dav1d_decoder.cc](https://webrtc.googlesource.com/src/+/main/modules/video_coding/codecs/av1/dav1d_decoder.cc)), so 10-bit AV1 there fails whenever Chrome falls back to software. One caution comes from presentation, not decode: moonlight-qt's libplacebo renderer disables 10-bit SDR output because "some drivers don't dither 10-bit SDR output correctly" ([plvk.cpp](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt/blob/master/app/streaming/video/ffmpeg-renderers/plvk.cpp)).

### Two SDR colour questions need answers before either

The first is a possible existing bug. Cha feeds NVENC ARGB and tags BT.709 primaries and matrix with the sRGB transfer, limited range, in the H.264/HEVC VUI and the AV1 colour config [C] ([`nvenc.rs`](../../../crates/cha-nvenc/src/nvenc.rs)). FFmpeg, for every packed RGB input to NVENC, hard-codes the matrix as BT.470BG (BT.601) and limited range, from a 2022 change reportedly saying NVENC ignores the VUI in its built-in conversion ([FFmpeg nvenc.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/master/libavcodec/nvenc.c); the commit's wording was seen only in a search summary of [ffmpeg-cvslog](https://lists.mplayerhq.hu/pipermail/ffmpeg-cvslog/2022-August/133327.html)) [D], one developer reports the RGB formats give "no control over the chromaticity coordinates" [D] ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/dxgi-nvenc-yuv444-10bit-format-compatability/189782)), and Sunshine never feeds NVENC RGB at all ([nvenc_base.cpp](https://github.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/blob/master/src/nvenc/nvenc_base.cpp)). If NVENC converts with BT.601 coefficients and the decoder inverts with BT.709, greys are correct and saturated reds and greens shift [E]. Nobody has checked this on Cha's hardware, so it is a test, not a fact: encode solid sRGB primary and secondary patches and compare the decoded RGB under each matrix tag.

The second is a disagreement between Cha's own encoders [C]. NVENC tags transfer 13 (sRGB) because a BT.709 tag made Chrome on a Mac show midtones "about 11 levels too light", per the comment in [`nvenc.rs`](../../../crates/cha-nvenc/src/nvenc.rs); but the VA-API H.264 and HEVC VUI, the VA-API AV1 sequence header ([`bitstream/av1.rs`](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/encoder/bitstream/av1.rs)), x264 and SVT-AV1 all tag transfer 1, and the CPU path's [`i420.rs`](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/encoder/i420.rs) converts the matrix without changing the curve. CPU-node streams therefore probably show the same 11-level shift on a Mac [E]. VA-API depends on whether Mesa's video processor re-encodes the curve: Mesa resolves Cha's `SRGB` surface standard to a gamma-2.2 transfer and the BT.709 output to BT.709 ([Mesa postproc.c](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/raw/main/src/gallium/frontends/va/postproc.c)), and whether its shader path then converts between them is unknown. A grey ramp per encoder on Chrome macOS settles both. The same pass should check the native player: wgpu's unreleased trunk fixes "oversaturated sRGB surface colors on wide-gamut displays by explicitly setting the layer's sRGB color space" (#10286) ([wgpu CHANGELOG](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/CHANGELOG.md)), so Cha Player on wgpu 29 may be oversaturating SDR on the MacBook's P3 panel today [E].

## HDR on the node needs a colour pipeline Smithay does not have

### Game sessions: gamescope offers HDR only when Cha reports headroom

In Cha's setup, Proton games present through Wine's X11 driver to gamescope's Xwayland, and gamescope decides everything. Its Vulkan WSI layer adds the HDR10 (`A2B10G10R10`/`A2R10G10B10` with `HDR10_ST2084`) and scRGB (`R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT` with `EXTENDED_SRGB_LINEAR`) swapchain formats only when the `GAMESCOPE_HDR_OUTPUT_FEEDBACK` root property is 1 ([WSI layer](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/layer/VkLayer_FROG_gamescope_wsi.cpp)); that needs `--hdr-enabled` and an HDR-capable connector ([steamcompmgr.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/steamcompmgr.cpp)); and a nested connector counts as HDR-capable only when the host's preferred image description has a target maximum luminance above its reference luminance, both defaulting to 203 nits, since 3.16.18 ([gamescope 7d4e835](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/commit/7d4e835)). DXVK, which vkd3d-proton's DXGI shares, tells the game Windows HDR is on only with `DXVK_HDR=1` ([DXVK dxgi_options.cpp](https://github.com/doitsujin/dxvk/blob/master/src/dxgi/dxgi_options.cpp)); Valve's Proton launchers have no HDR switch ([Proton 11](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/Proton/blob/proton_11.0/proton)), while GE-Proton detects HDR from gamescope's feedback and says to keep Wine's default XWayland driver inside gamescope ([GE-Proton README](https://github.com/GloriousEggroll/proton-ge-custom/blob/master/README.md)). So on Valve Proton Cha sets `DXVK_HDR=1` itself, and never `PROTON_ENABLE_WAYLAND`. No monitor or EDID is involved: the nested connector always synthesises the EDID it gives Xwayland ([WaylandBackend.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/Backends/WaylandBackend.cpp)). Which atom the closed Steam client reads before it shows its HDR toggle is unverified; `GAMESCOPE_DISPLAY_SUPPORTS_HDR` is the likely one.

The host-protocol gate is strict. gamescope binds `frog_color_management_v1` and `wp_color_manager_v1` at version 1, not `color-representation`, and its `wp` path requires six features (parametric, set_primaries, set_mastering_display_primaries, extended_target_volume, set_luminances, windows_scrgb), the PQ transfer and the sRGB and BT.2020 primaries ([WaylandBackend.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/Backends/WaylandBackend.cpp)). **Ubuntu's 3.16.20, Cha's base, also requires the deprecated `srgb` transfer**, as does Ubuntu's newer 3.16.24; upstream dropped it only in 3.16.25 ([3.16.20 source](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/3.16.20/src/Backends/WaylandBackend.cpp); [f1156ce](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/commit/f1156ce); [Launchpad](https://launchpad.net/ubuntu/+source/gamescope)). That gate broke nested HDR on Plasma 6.7, which advertises no `srgb` transfer ([gamescope #2221](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/issues/2221)), and keeps the `wp` path off under GNOME, whose Mutter lacks three of the features ([Mutter](https://gitlab.gnome.org/GNOME/mutter/-/blob/main/src/wayland/meta-wayland-color-management.c)). The frog protocol is smaller, but gamescope creates its planes through `wp` whenever a `wp_color_manager_v1` global is bound, so that route would mean hiding `wp` from gamescope's connection while showing it to Chrome. The simpler route is a complete `wp_color_manager_v1` (offering `srgb` to version-1 binders is allowed, since it is deprecated only "since 2") plus the gamescope 3.17.0 rebuild, which drops the `srgb` requirement and adds a content-driven HDR mode in which the output is PQ only while a live window has an HDR commit, with the feedback property reporting capability rather than state because "the WSI layer reads it once at surface creation" ([gamescope 6513879](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/commit/6513879)). On a node, HDR should follow the game, not a panel toggle, so that is the behaviour Cha wants.

What arrives is not one buffer. In the passthrough case gamescope forwards the game's own swapchain image as a subsurface, FP16 scRGB tagged `windows_scrgb` or 10-bit PQ tagged BT.2020/PQ with the game's mastering metadata, next to SDR planes for the Steam overlay and mangoapp; only when it must composite (FSR or NIS, blur, fades, inverse tone mapping, or a host without colour management) does it send one BT.2020/PQ plane in `XBGR2101010` ([WaylandBackend.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/Backends/WaylandBackend.cpp); [rendervulkan.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/rendervulkan.cpp)). Cha must therefore composite mixed image descriptions surface by surface, and pick a single owner for SDR-in-HDR, because gamescope's own `--hdr-sdr-content-nits` (default 400) applies only when gamescope composites ([main.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/main.cpp)). Whether `--mangoapp` forces a full composite is unknown. The debug flags are not shortcuts for production: `--hdr-debug-force-support` lets games pick HDR swapchains but outputs SDR, and `--hdr-debug-force-output` "will look very wrong" unless the host expects PQ ([main.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/main.cpp)). The second is still useful as a measurement spike, with Cha treating gamescope's toplevel as BT.2020/PQ by convention, to get real PQ game frames into the encoder before the protocol exists [E].

### Desktop sessions: Chrome and KWin follow whatever Cha describes

Chromium binds `wp_color_manager_v1` by default, needs parametric descriptions, treats an output as HDR when target maximum over reference luminance exceeds 1, and then draws into `RGBA_F16` if the compositor's dmabuf formats include it, otherwise `RGBA_1010102` ([wayland_wp_color_manager.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/ui/ozone/platform/wayland/host/wayland_wp_color_manager.cc); [wayland_wp_image_description.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/ui/ozone/platform/wayland/host/wayland_wp_image_description.cc); [wayland_screen.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/ui/ozone/platform/wayland/host/wayland_screen.cc)); its Wayland HDR has shipped washed-out output on Hyprland and blurry text on KDE [D] ([Hyprland #11910](https://github.com/hyprwm/Hyprland/discussions/11910)). Firefox keeps Linux HDR behind `gfx.color_management.hdr`, needs parametric, BT.2020 and PQ plus the WebRender native compositor, and uses it for video overlays only ([StaticPrefList.yaml](https://github.com/mozilla-firefox/firefox/blob/main/modules/libpref/init/StaticPrefList.yaml); [nsWaylandDisplay.cpp](https://github.com/mozilla-firefox/firefox/blob/main/widget/gtk/nsWaylandDisplay.cpp)). Nested KWin since Plasma 6.4 forwards HDR by adopting the host's preferred description and encoding gamma 2.2 over an extended luminance range, so Cha must honour `set_luminances` on gamma-2.2 descriptions instead of treating gamma 2.2 as SDR ([KWin wayland_output.cpp](https://invent.kde.org/plasma/kwin/-/blob/master/src/backends/wayland/wayland_output.cpp)).

| Client | What it needs from Cha's compositor |
|---|---|
| gamescope ≤ 3.16.24 | all six features, PQ, sRGB and BT.2020 primaries, the `srgb` transfer |
| gamescope ≥ 3.16.25 | the same without `srgb` |
| Chromium | parametric and an output description with max > reference; FP16 or 10-bit dmabuf formats |
| Firefox | parametric, BT.2020, PQ, and the pref set in the image |
| nested KWin | parametric, set_primaries, set_luminances, gamma 2.2 |
| GE-Proton's Wine Wayland path | max > reference |

The consequence for desktops is that advertising headroom switches every Chrome window in the session to FP16 rendering, so desktop HDR has to be a per-session decision taken only for an HDR client. Smithay's maintainer gave the governing rule for a streaming compositor: "if the encoder only accepts 8-bit input, the protocol should not be advertised" ([Wolf #222](https://github.com/games-on-whales/wolf/issues/222)).

### The protocol code exists; the composite and the encoder hand-off do not

Upstream Smithay has no colour-management module, and PR #1143 is a 2023 draft described as "completely untested, just compiles" ([Smithay #1143](https://github.com/Smithay/smithay/pull/1143)); niri and cosmic-comp have none either ([niri tty.rs](https://github.com/niri-wm/niri/blob/main/src/backend/tty.rs); [cosmic-comp #1384](https://github.com/pop-os/cosmic-comp/issues/1384)). The reusable code is MIT-licensed: `dividebysandwich/smithay`'s `ds-hdr-master` branch carries a **1,732-line `wp-color-management-v1` version-3 server module** plus color-representation, built on Smithay master of 2026-07-06 ([ds-hdr-master](https://github.com/dividebysandwich/smithay/tree/ds-hdr-master)), and `cosmic-hdr-reference` was validated on an RTX 5090 and a Radeon 680M, with one community report of an RTX 4090 running Cyberpunk 2077 in HDR10 under GE-Proton [D] ([cosmic-hdr-reference](https://github.com/franzjeger/cosmic-hdr-reference)). Cha's pin is newer than that branch's base, and Cha needs none of its DRM parts, so vendoring the protocol module into `cha-streamer` beats pinning a fork.

Rendering is Cha's to write. Smithay's GLES renderer already maps `Abgr2101010` to `RGB10_A2` and `Abgr16161616f` to `RGBA16F` and imports both ([gles/format.rs](https://github.com/Smithay/smithay/blob/master/src/backend/renderer/gles/format.rs)), but has no colour pipeline, and Cha's output pool allocates only `Xrgb8888`/`Argb8888` GBM buffers ([`output.rs` around line 332](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/compositor/output.rs)) [C]. The design the evidence supports is a linear-light `Abgr16161616f` composite, a texture shader per surface that decodes its image description (PQ, scRGB linear, gamma 2.2 with luminances for nested KWin, and SDR), SDR white mapped to a reference, and a single encode at the end. On blending the sources disagree: KWin blends in gamma 2.2 so translucency looks as it does in SDR ([Xaver Hugl](https://planet.kde.org/xavers-blog-2024-11-01-hdr-and-color-management-in-kwin-part-4-nonlinear-blending)), while Mutter requires FP16 for linear blending ([clutter-color-state-params.c](https://gitlab.gnome.org/GNOME/mutter/-/blob/main/clutter/clutter/clutter-color-state-params.c)) and cosmic-hdr-reference, which took an 8-bit SDR shortcut, says a production design "should composite all surfaces in a linear, high-precision intermediate". Linear FP16 wins for Cha, because game sessions have few translucent surfaces and desktops stay SDR by default, which removes KWin's reason. Its cost at 1440p and 4K at 120–240 Hz has not been measured.

The SDR decoding curve is a second disagreement. The protocol deprecates the `srgb` transfer as ambiguous and recommends gamma 2.2 ([color-management-v1.xml](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/wayland/wayland-protocols/-/blob/main/staging/color-management/color-management-v1.xml)), KWin decodes sRGB as gamma 2.2 ([colorspace.cpp](https://invent.kde.org/plasma/kwin/-/blob/master/src/core/colorspace.cpp)), Windows' SDR-in-HDR uses the piecewise curve, and users dispute KWin's choice ([KDE bug 515402](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=515402)). Cha's own evidence decides it for Cha: tagging the SDR stream with the sRGB transfer is what made Chrome on a Mac show the desktop's levels correctly [C], so Cha's main client decodes desktop pixels with the piecewise curve. SDR surfaces composited into an HDR stream should be decoded with the curve Cha's SDR stream already implies to that client, so that switching a session to HDR does not move its shadows, and a near-black ramp compared between an SDR and an HDR session checks it [E]. For the reference white, BT.2408-9 (March 2026) puts HDR reference, diffuse and graphics white at **203 cd/m², 58% of the PQ signal** ([ITU-R BT.2408-9](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-BT.2408-9-2026-PDF-E.pdf)), the W3C draft anchors SDR content there ([CSS Color HDR](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-hdr/)), and Chromium maps PQ 0.581 to SDR white ([Chromium 4ec54d51](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/4ec54d519e9469ab0f6d010b51da89914fc009ad)); 203 should be the default, replaced by the client's own SDR white when the client reports it.

The hand-off to the encoder is the hard part. CUDA 13.0's `CUeglColorFormat` has no packed 10-bit RGB or FP16 entry ([CUDA driver API](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/13.0.3/cuda-driver-api/group__CUDA__TYPES.html)), so registering a 10-bit EGL image with CUDA is unproven. Cha already met the 8-bit version of this problem ("CUDA's EGL interop won't import an R8 image") and planned a Vulkan compute pass that writes NV12 into CUDA external memory for NVENC to read as `CUDADEVICEPTR` ([streamer README](../../../crates/cha-streamer/README.md)) [C], and the plan's PyroWave section already calls for "one scaled encode that does RGB→YCbCr, crop and scale, and HDR10" ([PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) §3.3) [C]. HDR extends that pass: import the FP16 composite, apply PQ and the BT.2020 non-constant-luminance matrix, and write P010 for NVENC's `YUV420_10BIT`, for a VA-API P010 surface, and for PyroWave. Mesa's VA frontend also imports `AR30`/`AB30`/`XR30`/`XB30` dmabufs ([va_private.h](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/blob/main/src/gallium/frontends/va/va_private.h)), so AMD could take 10-bit RGB directly. Whether NVIDIA's GBM allocates renderable `ABGR16161616F` or `ABGR2101010` buffers with block-linear modifiers on the 4090 is unverified; gamescope notes that NVIDIA lacks linear `A2R10G10B10` storage images and writes `XBGR` order instead ([rendervulkan.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/rendervulkan.cpp)).

Prior art is thin, and the notes disagree about it. The node researcher found no Smithay-based streamer compositing HDR: Wolf's merged gst-wayland-display PR #37 adds P010 and 10-bit caps but no PQ design, with the compositor waiting on Smithay #1143 ([PR #37](https://github.com/games-on-whales/gst-wayland-display/pull/37); [Wolf #222](https://github.com/games-on-whales/wolf/issues/222)); Selkies' pixelflux encodes 10-bit with no PQ handling documented ([LinuxServer](https://docs.linuxserver.io/selkies/components/pixelflux/)); Sunshine does Linux HDR only through KMS capture of a real connector's `HDR_OUTPUT_METADATA` ([kmsgrab.cpp](https://github.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/blob/master/src/platform/linux/kmsgrab.cpp)), which a containerised node without host privileges cannot use; and gamescope's PipeWire stream offers only BGRx and NV12 at BT.601/709 ([pipewire.cpp](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/gamescope/blob/master/src/pipewire.cpp)), so Steam's 22 October beta's "HDR capture on Linux desktops with PipeWire" does not cover gamescope ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/10/steam-client-beta-october-22nd-revamps-remote-play-hdr-support/)). The node note also found nothing on Nestri, but the industry researcher found Nestri PRs that "make HDR reachable", verified a 10-bit HDR capture path "end to end", and promoted it to production on 2026-09-28 ([nestri #314](https://github.com/nestrilabs/nestri/pull/314); [#315](https://github.com/nestrilabs/nestri/pull/315); [#346](https://github.com/nestrilabs/nestri/pull/346)). Specific merged PRs outweigh a search that came back empty, so Nestri is the first prior art to read; whether its compositor manages colour per surface or forwards one PQ buffer was not checked.

## Encoders take P010 and static metadata, each in its own way

### NVENC needs Cha's own BT.2020 conversion

[`crates/cha-nvenc/src/sys.rs`](../../../crates/cha-nvenc/src/sys.rs) is NVENC API 13.0 and already contains `NV_ENC_BUFFER_FORMAT_ABGR10`, `NV_ENC_BUFFER_FORMAT_YUV420_10BIT` and the `outputMaxCll`/`outputMasteringDisplay` bitfields [C], so HDR10 needs no SDK upgrade. NVENC accepts packed 10-bit RGB, but its RGB→YUV conversion is an internal CUDA kernel whose matrix NVIDIA does not document [V] ([NVENC guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/video-codec-sdk/13.0/nvenc-video-encoder-api-prog-guide/index.html)), FFmpeg tags it BT.601, and one developer got correct HDR from `ABGR10` with HEVC Main10 only after setting the VUI to BT.2020/SMPTE 2084/BT.2020-NCL by hand [D] ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nvenc-hdr10/351194)). Cha should feed NVENC P010 from its own conversion, as Sunshine feeds it from its own CUDA kernel ([Sunshine cuda.cu](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/master/src/platform/linux/cuda.cu)), and tag 9/16/9. The extra kernel replaces NVENC's own, so the GPU cost should be similar [E]. Static metadata is native for HEVC and AV1 from SDK 13.0 through the config flags and the per-picture `pMasteringDisplay`/`pMaxCll` pointers ([nvEncodeAPI.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFmpeg/nv-codec-headers/master/include/ffnvcodec/nvEncodeAPI.h)); [`crates/cha-gamestream/src/hdr.rs`](../../../crates/cha-gamestream/src/hdr.rs), ported from Moonshine, already injects HDR10 MDCV/CLLI SEI and AV1 metadata OBUs [C] and stays useful for older SDKs. Whether NVENC repeats the metadata on later IDRs is unverified, and with infinite GOP Cha must make sure it is on the first IDR and on every RFI-forced one. Desktop 4:4:4 HDR on NVENC means HEVC Rext, since the AV1 config still says "yuv444 input currently not supported" in SDK 13.1 ([nvEncodeAPI.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFmpeg/nv-codec-headers/master/include/ffnvcodec/nvEncodeAPI.h)). Nobody publishes 10-bit encode latency: NVIDIA's only throughput table is 8-bit 1080p (HEVC P1 low-latency CBR at 1055 fps per Ada engine, AV1 at 1090) [V] ([NVENC application note](https://docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/video-codec-sdk/13.0/nvenc-application-note/index.html)), and an independent latency study converted its HDR sources to SDR and listed HDR as future work [I] ([arXiv 2511.18688](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18688v2)). Cha measured 1.6–2.4 ms p50 at 1440p for 8-bit on the 4090 [C] ([streamer README](../../../crates/cha-streamer/README.md)); the same test with P010 input is the number to get.

### VA-API splits by GPU generation

| Node GPU | HEVC Main10 encode | AV1 10-bit encode | 10-bit RGB → P010 on the GPU | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intel UHD 630 (Kaby/Coffee Lake) | no | no | VPP cannot output P010 | SDR only |
| Intel Ice Lake | P010 input | no | VPP takes no `A2R10G10B10` input and refuses BT.2020 in its composition path | Cha's shader to P010 |
| Intel Tiger Lake and later | P010, or VDEnc "RGB" input (format unverified) | Arc, Meteor/Lunar Lake, Battlemage, Panther Lake: P010 | VEBOX/SFC with explicit 9/16/9, untested | Cha's shader first |
| AMD VCN ≥ 2.0 / ≥ 4.0 (780M) | yes | VCN ≥ 4.0 | EFC in the encoder: no scaling, equal input and output primaries and transfer, no DCC | EFC or Cha's shader |

The Intel rows come from its feature tables ([media_features.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/intel/media-driver/master/docs/media_features.md)) and the AMD row from Mesa ([ac_video.c](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/raw/main/src/amd/common/ac_video.c); [radeon_vcn_enc.c](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/raw/main/src/gallium/drivers/radeonsi/mm/radeon_vcn_enc.c)). Cha's video processor sets an `SRGB` surface standard and a `BT709` output ([`vaapi/ffi.rs`](../../../crates/cha-streamer/src/encoder/vaapi/ffi.rs)) [C], which Mesa resolves to unequal transfers, so the 780M probably does not use EFC today even for SDR [E]. Explicit colour properties with equal transfers in and out (for HDR, 9/16/0 RGB in and 9/16/9 out) and a buffer without DCC would qualify; explicit values also avoid Mesa mapping `VAProcColorStandardBT2020` to PQ where libva documents transfer 14 ([postproc.c](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/raw/main/src/gallium/frontends/va/postproc.c); [va_vpp.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/intel/libva/master/va/va_vpp.h)). Neither driver takes MDCV or CLL as an encode parameter, so Cha writes the SEI and OBUs itself, as FFmpeg's VA-API encoders do ([vaapi_encode_h265.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/master/libavcodec/vaapi_encode_h265.c)). The failure reports are real: on an RX 9070 XT the radeonsi encoders signalled BT.2020/PQ while "HDR colours were wrong" and SDR was right [D] ([Vibepollo #707](https://github.com/Nonary/Vibepollo/issues/707)), and a Mesa maintainer said only P010 is supported for 10-bit encoding [D] ([Sunshine #1811](https://github.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/issues/1811)).

### CPU nodes stay SDR, and PyroWave is the cheapest HDR on the node

No one has published a low-delay SVT-AV1 comparison of 8 and 10 bits; the one hard data point is a regression report in which 10-bit throughput fell from about 100 to 34 fps at preset 10, in a random-access configuration [D] ([SVT-AV1 #1979](https://gitlab.com/AOMediaCodec/SVT-AV1/-/issues/1979)). Cha's CPU AV1 already holds 1440p60 on a desktop picture only "at the edge" (16 ms of 16.7) [C] ([streamer README](../../../crates/cha-streamer/README.md)), so CPU-node HDR waits for a measurement. x264's High 10 has no browser hardware-decode path the researchers could find, and NVENC encodes 10-bit H.264 only on Blackwell [V] ([NVENC guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/video-codec-sdk/13.0/nvenc-video-encoder-api-prog-guide/index.html)), so 10-bit H.264 is out.

PyroWave takes HDR almost for free on the node. Its encoder accepts sRGB, scRGB linear and HDR10 PQ input, outputs sRGB or HDR10 PQ with the BT.2020 matrix ("HDR10 is *not* tonemapped"), and says an R16 intermediate is "more or less required for HDR10" ([pyrowave.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Themaister/pyrowave/master/pyrowave.h)). Cha's PyroWave sequence header already carries primaries, transfer (BT.709 or PQ), YCbCr transform, range and chroma siting (`ColorInfo` in [`parser.rs`](../../../crates/cha-pyrowave-wgpu/src/parser.rs)) [C], but [`cha-pyrowave`](../../../crates/cha-pyrowave/src/lib.rs) imports one plane of 8-bit RGB(A) and the render shaders apply the BT.2020 matrix, clamp, and have no PQ handling [C]. Vibepollo's guidance prices 10 bits at ×1.15 the bits of 8 [D] ([pyrowave-protocol.md](https://github.com/Nonary/Vibepollo/blob/master/docs/pyrowave-protocol.md)), and Steam already ships PyroWave with HDR chosen automatically on wired LANs [V] ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Valve-Steam-Beta-Pyrowave)).

| PyroWave mode [E, from 1.6 bits/pixel × Vibepollo's factors] | 1440p60 | 1440p120 | 2160p60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8-bit 4:2:0 (1.6 bpp) | ≈354 Mbit/s | ≈708 Mbit/s | ≈796 Mbit/s |
| 10-bit 4:2:0 (1.84 bpp) | ≈407 Mbit/s | ≈814 Mbit/s | ≈916 Mbit/s |
| 10-bit 4:4:4 (2.94 bpp) | ≈650 Mbit/s | ≈1.3 Gbit/s | ≈1.47 Gbit/s |

The arithmetic matches Cha's own experience: 8-bit 4:4:4 at 1440p60 works out to about 566 Mbit/s, and C2.3's 4:4:4 at 580 Mbit/s saturated the owner's link [C] ([PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md)). On 1 GbE, PyroWave HDR means 10-bit 4:2:0 at 1440p60; 4:4:4 HDR and 1440p120 HDR need 2.5 GbE or more [E]. Whether the encoder takes an FP16 (`SFLOAT`) view for scRGB, given its "RGB(A) UNORM" wording, is unclear.

### HDR10 with static metadata is the only format worth sending

Every game-streaming stack with documented HDR sends HDR10 — BT.2020 primaries, PQ, the BT.2020-NCL matrix, 10 bits, static metadata — and Sunshine selects it only when the client asks and the host display is in HDR ([video_colorspace.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LizardByte/Sunshine/master/src/video_colorspace.cpp)). Moonlight carries the metadata out of band in `SS_HDR_METADATA` as well as in band ([Limelight.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-common-c/master/src/Limelight.h)); Chrome ignores HDR10+ SEI and plays HDR10 [D] ([StaZhu](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StaZhu/enable-chromium-hevc-hardware-decoding/main/README.md)); and HLG's one advantage, SDR compatibility, is moot when every client is negotiated, while its scene-referred OOTF would need a peak-dependent conversion on the node [E]. The mastering display should be the compositor's target (the client's display, once reported); content light level cannot be known in real time, so send the target peak or omit CLLI, but never zeros, which sinks treat inconsistently (Chrome 108 cut MaxCLL low when metadata was absent [D]). For bits, Apple's HLS ladder budgets HDR at about **1.2×** SDR at every rung from 540p up [V] ([Apple HLS spec](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/http-live-streaming/hls-authoring-specification-for-apple-devices)), PyroWave guidance at 1.15× [D], and GeForce NOW's bandwidth table, Sony's 38 Mbit/s 4K figure and moonlight-qt's default-bitrate formula add nothing for HDR ([GFN requirements](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/products/geforce-now/system-reqs); [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/10/11/ps5-cloud-streaming-launches-this-month-for-playstation-plus-premium-members/)); a widely repeated 30–40% figure has no source. Cha should budget ×1.15–1.25, starting at 1.25 because a one-frame VBV has no lookahead to absorb highlight detail [E], and never send PQ at 8 bits, which BT.2100 does not define.

Every layer has to agree: the VUI or AV1 colour config (9/16/9, limited range), the MDCV/CLLI SEI or metadata OBU, the WebCodecs `VideoDecoderConfig.colorSpace`, a metadata message on `cha-stream/1`, and, for the Moonlight host, `SS_HDR_METADATA`. moonlight-qt takes HDR10 metadata from the host's control stream rather than from VideoToolbox attachments ([vt_base.mm](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt/blob/master/app/streaming/video/ffmpeg-renderers/vt_base.mm)), which is the safer contract for Cha too. Cha's GameStream backends always report `hdr: false` and the handoff returns 422 for HDR [C] ([streamer README](../../../crates/cha-streamer/README.md)); once the node produces HDR10, `hdr.rs` and the 0x010e packet can carry the same values. A mismatch between layers produces exactly the washed-out picture of Vibepollo #707 [D], and with infinite GOP every IDR and every RFI recovery must repeat headers and metadata.

## Each client path has a different HDR ceiling, and none is proven

### Chrome on the M4: `<video>` is fastest, the canvas is surest, WebRTC is weakest

| Path | How HDR could reach the XDR panel | What is unproven | Cost today [C] |
|---|---|---|---|
| WebTransport → WebCodecs → `MediaStreamTrackGenerator` → `<video>` | Chrome decodes HEVC Main10 in hardware; its VideoToolbox decoder copies the bitstream's colour space and HDR metadata onto every frame and tags the IOSurface ([video_toolbox_video_decoder.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/media/gpu/mac/video_toolbox_video_decoder.cc)) | whether a PQ frame written into the generator shows EDR; a colour-space disagreement silently drops the overlay | ~3 ms send→compositor |
| PyroWave → WebGPU canvas | `rgba16float` with `toneMapping: {mode: "extended"}`, shipped in Chrome 128/129 ([blink-dev](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/rBQIRHUEAe8); [New in WebGPU 129](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-129)), with Cha's shader doing PQ and roll-off | FP16 present cost | 4–5 ms more than `<video>` |
| WebRTC → `<video>` | only through the hardware decoder adapter, which reinitialises on a colour-space change ([rtc_video_decoder_adapter.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/peerconnection/rtc_video_decoder_adapter.cc)) | Main10 in Chrome's offer; str0m and the colour-space header extension | n/a |

The middle row is the surest: Cha's canvas today is configured with `navigator.gpu.getPreferredCanvasFormat()`, `alphaMode: "opaque"` and no `toneMapping` ([`pyro.ts` lines 79–80](../../../web/packages/player/src/pyro.ts)) [C], which clamps to SDR. Reconfiguring it to `rgba16float` with extended tone mapping, checking `getConfiguration()`, then applying the PQ EOTF, dividing by 203 nits, converting BT.2020 to P3 or sRGB primaries with negatives kept, re-encoding with the sign-preserving sRGB curve and rolling off highlights in Cha's own shader (no headroom API has shipped) is a recipe the WebGPU spec supports, since extended values "are unchanged" and conversions do not clamp ([WebGPU ED](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/)). The FP16 back buffer at 3024×1964 is about 48 MB against 24 MB, roughly 5.7 GB/s written at 120 fps, small next to the M4's memory bandwidth, so the risk is an overlay or format penalty in the compositor rather than bandwidth [E]. The first row is the fastest and the least certain: the plumbing suggests a PQ `VideoFrame` keeps its colour space and metadata into the generator, but no source confirms EDR display, and the decoder removes `SHARED_IMAGE_USAGE_SCANOUT` whenever Chrome and macOS disagree on the colour space, which would force composition. The third row fails on the software fallbacks: dav1d accepts only 8 bits, HEVC "does not have SW fallback", Chrome's WebRTC HEVC intent never mentions Main10 ([blink-dev](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/3h8lL8a377c)), and the colour-space RTP header extension that carries metadata in its two-byte form is still marked experimental ([libwebrtc](https://webrtc.googlesource.com/src/+/main/docs/native-code/rtp-hdrext/color-space/README.md)).

HDR and WebGPU post-processing of decoded video do not mix yet. `importExternalTexture` zero-copies only NV12 frames into sRGB or P3, with HDR support a TODO ([external_texture_helper.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/webgpu/external_texture_helper.cc)), and GPU-backed 10-bit frames report `format: null` and refuse `copyTo()` ([video_frame.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/webcodecs/video_frame.cc)), so HDR frames stay on the plain `<video>` path, and the SGSR sharpen pass and note 11's WebCodecs upscaling prototype stay off whenever HDR is on. Detection is coarse but sufficient: `matchMedia('(dynamic-range: high)')` decides whether to ask for HDR, `isConfigSupported` with the 10-bit strings gates the WebCodecs path, and `getConfiguration().toneMapping` gates the canvas. The screen's live headroom is still behind a flag in a fingerprinting debate ([public-colorweb](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-colorweb/2025Jun/0000.html)), and a page-level `dynamic-range-limit: standard` on any ancestor would quietly force SDR, so the player should set `no-limit` on its `<video>` and canvas explicitly ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/dynamic-range-limit)). No cloud service ships browser HDR: GeForce NOW gives browsers 10-bit SDR at most and Mac browsers nothing ([GFN release highlights](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce-now/release-highlights/)), so on Chrome macOS Cha would be first, and should expect no one else's numbers.

### Windows Chrome before Safari, Firefox last

The requested order puts Safari second, but the evidence puts Windows Chrome ahead of it. Chrome on Windows decodes HEVC Main10 in hardware, has allowed `R10G10B10A2` HDR10 swap chains for BT.2020/PQ video without metadata since 2019 "because HDR metadata is an optional extension for WebRTC streams" ([Chromium 4130a38e](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/4130a38e27c173f4789fd8b87e73ae082e1599e2)), and is where GeForce NOW already ships 10-bit browser streams [V]. Safari is blocked on WebKit, not on Cha. Its 26.0 notes claim "HDR support in WebGPU Canvas" ([Safari 26 notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/safari-release-notes/safari-26-release-notes)), but a Google engineer saw macOS accept `extended` without displaying it in 2025, and no later confirmation exists ([WebKit 272702](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=272702)); WebKit misreads 10-bit hardware frames in `importExternalTexture` [D] ([Silo-Server #2544](https://github.com/Silo-Server/silo-server/pull/2544)); Safari has no main-thread track generator, and WebKit's own `VideoTrackGenerator`-to-`<video>` test failed constantly on Sequoia ([WebKit 289211](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=289211)); and, per [note 05](../05-web-transport-and-input.md), WebKit's WebTransport deadlocks after about 16 MiB, which leaves Safari on WebRTC, the weakest HDR path. Firefox comes last: its WebCodecs `VideoFrame.colorSpace` is guessed for every software-decoded frame ([Mozilla 2057759](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2057759)), `VideoTrackGenerator` is unshipped ([Mozilla 1749533](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1749533)), and its Windows HDR video, shipped in mid-2026, makes "no attempt … to read and use HDR metadata" ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260715-firefox-windows-hdr-video/)).

### The native player should tone-map in its own shader

macOS offers two ways to show HDR10 from a custom Metal layer, and the evidence favours the one that costs no extra pass.

| | (a) Extended linear, Cha tone-maps | (b) PQ passthrough, macOS tone-maps |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | `Rgba16Float` + `ExtendedSrgbLinear` (EDR) | `Rgb10a2Unorm` + `kCGColorSpaceITUR_2100_PQ` + `CAEDRMetadata` (`opticalOutputScale` 203) |
| Who uses it | Apple's WWDC21 recipe [V] ([WWDC21 10161](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10161/)); NuvioDesktop's fix [D] | moonlight-qt ([vt_metal.mm](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt/blob/master/app/streaming/video/ffmpeg-renderers/vt_metal.mm)) |
| Cost and risk | one shader pass Cha already runs | "an extra processing pass. This, in turn, increases latency and bandwidth" [V]; near black when headroom is 1.0 on macOS 26.6.2 [D] ([NuvioDesktop #1042](https://github.com/NuvioMedia/NuvioDesktop/pull/1042)) |
| wgpu 30.0.1 | works (static colour-space constant) | `Bt2100Pq` crashes; the fix (#10175) is on trunk, its backport (#10672) open since 2026-10-20 ([wgpu #10672](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/pull/10672)) |

Path (a) should be the default and (b) an A/B comparison. The shader takes YCbCr through the BT.2020 matrix, applies the PQ EOTF, divides by a reference white, converts BT.2020 to BT.709 with negative values carrying the wider gamut, and rolls highlights off to the live `maximumExtendedDynamicRangeColorComponentValue`, which "may dynamically change" with brightness and above which values clip [V] ([WWDC21 10161](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10161/)); wgpu 30 reads it as `Surface::display_hdr_info`, on the main thread only ([wgpu-hal metal/surface.rs](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/wgpu-hal/src/metal/surface.rs)). On reference white the sources disagree: Apple's 2021 talk divides PQ by 100 nits, while moonlight-qt and NuvioDesktop map 203 nits to EDR 1.0, as BT.2408 and Chromium do. 203 wins for Cha, because it puts a streamed desktop's or game UI's white at macOS's own SDR white [E]; it should be one constant, A/B-tested on the panel. Because the MacBook Pro's SDR brightness reaches 1000 nits outdoors against a 1600-nit HDR peak ([Apple specs](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/specs/)), headroom can approach 1 at high brightness, so the player should stay in EDR and compress harder while potential headroom exceeds 1, as Cyberpunk 2077 on Mac polls live headroom and enables HDR when potential exceeds 2.0 [V] ([WWDC26 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/356/)), and fall back to tone-mapping into today's `Bgra8Unorm` surface otherwise.

The work is mostly migration. wgpu moves from 29.0.x to 30.0.1 and egui/egui-wgpu from 0.35 to 0.36 or 0.37, the first egui-wgpu releases on wgpu 30 ([crates.io](https://crates.io/crates/egui-wgpu)), with `Queue::present` and `create_texture_from_hal`'s new `initial_state` to migrate ([wgpu CHANGELOG](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/CHANGELOG.md)). egui writes gamma-encoded values into a non-sRGB target ([egui-wgpu renderer.rs](https://github.com/emilk/egui/blob/main/crates/egui-wgpu/src/renderer.rs)), which an extended-linear layer would read as linear [E], so egui renders into a fixed 8-bit offscreen texture composited at EDR 1.0. [`render/video.wgsl`](../../../crates/cha-player/src/render/video.wgsl) clamps RGB to [0,1] [C] and has to stop for HDR, as must `cha-upscale` and the PyroWave render pass. On the decoder side, besides the 10-bit format request, the player should read `kCVImageBufferTransferFunctionKey` and the mastering and light-level attachments, a big-endian copy of the SEI payload [V] ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corevideo/kcvimagebuffermasteringdisplaycolorvolumekey.md)); whether VideoToolbox sets them for a stream created from parameter sets is unverified, which is another reason to carry the metadata on `cha-stream/1`. The later Windows player has a matching recipe — FP16 scRGB for general apps, where 1.0 means 80 nits and SDR is multiplied by `SdrWhiteLevel/80`, or `R10G10B10A2` with `SetColorSpace1(G2084_NONE_P2020)` for video [V] ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3darticles/high-dynamic-range)) — and wgpu 30 covers both on DX12 but has no `SetHDRMetaData`. The Steam Deck OLED's 1000-nit peak over 600 nits SDR gives only about 1.7× headroom ([Steam Deck specs](https://www.steamdeck.com/en/tech)) [E].

## SDR content and SDR clients decide whether HDR feels broken

The industry's record says HDR's costs are engineering, not bits. GeForce NOW is the only service with both HDR and VRR, and only in its native apps ([GFN requirements](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/products/geforce-now/system-reqs)); Parsec streams 8-bit Rec. 709 and tells users to turn HDR off ([Parsec](https://support.parsec.app/hc/en-us/articles/32361382749204-Color-Management-Workflow-In-Parsec)); Apollo says HDR "is generally not recommended with ANY streaming solutions at this moment" ([Apollo](https://github.com/ClassicOldSong/Apollo)); and the Moonlight trackers' HDR complaints are oversaturation, raised blacks and washed-out pictures, not bandwidth — tvOS ([#582](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-ios/issues/582)), an M4 iPad ([#667](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-ios/issues/667)), Linux Wayland ([#1875](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-qt/issues/1875)) [D]. Valve's 22 October 2026 beta gives the clearest precedent: an HDR setting of Automatic ("enabled when HDR content is enabled"), On and Off [V] ([Steam Deck HQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/steam-deck-gets-hdr-remote-play-overhaul-in-latest-beta-client-update/)). The guidance bodies agree on one tone-mapping pass, graphics white at 203 cd/m², and the client deciding final presentation, with KDE's developer advising native apps to "drop all HDR settings" and follow the compositor ([HGIG explained](https://www.whathifi.com/advice/hgig-explained-what-is-hgig-how-do-you-get-it-and-should-you-use-it); [BT.2408-9](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-BT.2408-9-2026-PDF-E.pdf); [Xaver Hugl](https://planet.kde.org/xavers-blog-2025-03-31-on-brightness-and-calibrating-your-displays)).

Those points make Cha's policy. A session goes HDR only when its content is HDR and the client's display is (`dynamic-range: high`, EDR potential above 1, or DXGI's colour space), which is Valve's Automatic. An SDR client gets SDR made on the node, either because the compositor stays SDR or because the same compute pass that writes P010 tone-maps to SDR first — one spline or BT.2390 curve, without the costly peak detection libplacebo uses, since the compositor knows the content's peak ([libplacebo](https://libplacebo.org/options/)) [E] — because browser tone mapping varies by platform and Firefox ignores the metadata. Because one encoder serves every viewer of an environment ([streamer README](../../../crates/cha-streamer/README.md), ADR 0009) [C], HDR is a per-environment mode: a second viewer on an SDR display needs a second, tone-mapped encode, as Wolf's draft per-client PR builds P010 for HDR clients and NV12 BT.709 for SDR ones ([Wolf #555](https://github.com/games-on-whales/wolf/pull/555)), or the environment falls back to SDR. The client should send its display's peak, minimum, SDR white, headroom and gamut at session start, so that the preferred image description games calibrate against is the viewer's own — the virtual-display version of HGIG [E]. Desktops stay SDR, ideally 10-bit 4:4:4 (HEVC Rext on NVENC, PyroWave 4:4:4), unless an HDR surface is on screen and the client is HDR, since HDR costs about 1.2× the bits and risks black-level and tone-mapping differences for no gain on text [E]. And the player gives per-client SDR-brightness and peak overrides and shows HDR, bit depth and transfer in its stats, which Moonlight users ask for ([moonlight-android #1431](https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-android/issues/1431)) [D], instead of calibration screens.

## A ranked plan: measure, fix the clocks and the tags, then build HDR in two halves

## Conclusion

The question arrived as two features, and the evidence turns it into one problem: who owns the clock. On a wired LAN the biggest VRR gain needs no VRR display at all, because both clocks that delay a game frame are Cha's own, on the node, and removing them helps every client, browsers included; the display-side VRR that the industry markets is a smaller, platform-specific bonus that only a native full-screen player can collect, and only if it presents each frame as it arrives rather than adding a paced queue in front of the panel. HDR runs the other way. Its bit cost is about 15–25%, trivial on a LAN, while its real costs are a colour pipeline that no upstream Smithay compositor has, encoder conversions that each vendor does differently, and a client presenter that has to know the viewer's display. Because one encoder serves every viewer of an environment, the decision to go HDR belongs to the environment, and the client's display, not the node's, should define what HDR means.

Cha would be among the first containerised Smithay streamers to composite HDR and ahead of every cloud service in browser HDR, so each step has to be justified by its own measurements rather than by parity. The deciding experiments are cheap and mostly need no node change — a timestamp in the compositor, a page playing a canned PQ clip, a set of colour patches, a Perfetto trace — and they come before any colour-management code. Two of them may expose problems in the SDR streams Cha ships today, which is reason enough to run them this week.
