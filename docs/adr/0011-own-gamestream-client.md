# 0011: Our own GameStream client, replacing moonlight-common-rust

- **Status:** accepted (2026-10-07). Changes the GameStream library choice in [0008](0008-moonlight-hosts-adopted-by-a-node.md) (gateway, node discovery and pairing) and [0010](0010-native-client-macos-first.md) (Cha Player's GameStream transport); both stay otherwise.
- **Context:** the first real stream from Cha Player to our node ended with `ReedSolomon(IncorrectShardSize)` after a burst of large frames with a lost packet. `moonlight-common-rust`'s video depacketizer recovers FEC over payloads only and gives missing shards empty buffers; real Moonlight (`moonlight-common-c`) and Sunshine compute FEC over whole packets from the RTP header and allocate full-size buffers. So its recovery fails against Sunshine-style hosts, ours included, in the gateway and the node as well as the player. The library also can't decrypt video, has no AV1 and no reference-frame invalidation, and its GPL licence is the one obstacle to the Mac App Store (ADR 0010). Meanwhile `cha-gamestream` (ADR 0009) already holds a tested, Sunshine-compatible implementation of most of the protocol: packet formats, FEC, AES-GCM/CBC, the control channel's framing, the pairing crypto.
- **Decision:**
  - **`cha-gamestream` gets a `client` half**, beside `front` and `media`, in the same style: a front client (serverinfo, the five-phase pairing, applist, appasset, launch, resume, cancel over HTTP/HTTPS with our client certificate; RTSP) and a media client (the ENet control channel with AES-GCM, video depacketizing with full-packet Reed-Solomon recovery and AES-GCM decryption, audio with RS(4,2) and AES-CBC, input encoding from `cha-gamestream`'s neutral `InputEvent`, feedback decoding), reaching the rest of the world through small traits and plain channels. It follows `moonlight-common-c`'s behaviour where the protocol is ambiguous.
  - **Every user switches to it:** Cha Player's transport (`cha-client-gamestream`), `cha-gateway`, and the node's Moonlight-host discovery and pairing. `cha-moonlight-input` maps the browser model to `cha_gamestream::InputEvent`. `moonlight-common-rust` then leaves every shipped crate; it stays only as a test-only dev-dependency of `cha-gamestream`, as an independent client to cross-check our host against.
  - **Tests are the proof:** our client against our host on loopback, a lossy UDP proxy dropping packets (single- and multi-block frames, the last shard of a block, parity), encrypted and unencrypted video, AV1 passthrough, golden packets shared with the host's packetizer tests; then a real Sunshine or Apollo host.
- **Consequences:** one GameStream implementation, ours, for host and client; FEC recovery that matches real hosts; video encryption, AV1 and RFI within reach on the client; no GPL code in the client, so the App Store needs only the maintainer's permission and contributors' ([0012](0012-cla-for-app-store-builds.md)). More protocol code to own, all in one crate with its tests.
