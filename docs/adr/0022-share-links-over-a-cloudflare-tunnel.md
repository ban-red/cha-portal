# 0022: Share links over a Cloudflare Tunnel, media over WebSocket

- **Status:** accepted (2026-10-08). Extends [0014](0014-share-links-for-players.md) and [0015](0015-share-links-for-viewers-and-controllers.md).
- **Context:** a share link only works for people who can reach the portal and the node: the LAN, the owner's tailnet, or a port-forward. Inviting a friend across the internet means opening ports on a home router, or running coturn with a port of its own. A Cloudflare Tunnel (`cloudflared`) needs no inbound port: it dials out to Cloudflare, which serves an HTTPS hostname and forwards requests and WebSockets back to a local port. It carries HTTP and WebSocket only, over TCP, so it can't carry WebRTC or WebTransport media, and the streamer's WebRTC is ICE-lite with host candidates only, so TURN somewhere on the internet can't reach a node behind NAT either. PLAN §3.1 already lists WebSocket as the last-resort carrier for `cha-stream/1`.
- **Decision:**
  - **An internet link is a share link with `wan: true`.** The owner ticks *Over the internet* in the Share dialog. Its URL is on the tunnel's hostname (`https://<host>/s/<token>`). Roles, limits, expiry and revocation are as in 0014/0015.
  - **The portal runs `cloudflared` itself, as a child process.** With `CHA_TUNNEL_TOKEN` (a named tunnel the owner made in their Cloudflare account) and `CHA_TUNNEL_HOSTNAME`, it runs that tunnel, and the URL is stable. Without them it opens a *quick tunnel* (`*.trycloudflare.com`, no account), whose hostname changes every time it starts, which ends the internet links made on the old one. A quick tunnel runs only while an internet link is live, and stops a minute after the last one ends; a named tunnel runs the same way. `CHA_TUNNEL=off` turns the feature off. The portal image ships `cloudflared` (Apache-2.0).
  - **The tunnel reaches a guest-only listener, never the portal.** The portal opens a second listener (`CHA_GUEST_LISTEN`, default `127.0.0.1:7680`) that serves the SPA's static files and the guest routes of internet links only (`/api/shares/{token}`, `…/connect`, `…/ice`, `/api/media/{ticket}`), and 404 for everything else: no sign-in, no dev login, no admin API, no node channel. Links not marked `wan` are unknown there. On this listener the client's address is `CF-Connecting-IP` (everything arrives from loopback), used for the rate limit and the audit log.
  - **Media goes over a WebSocket through the portal and the node.** A new transport, `websocket`: the portal hands the browser a one-use ticket (`/api/media/<ticket>`, 60 s). When the browser opens it, the portal asks the node over its channel to open a relay; the node dials the portal back on a WebSocket of its own (signed with its Ed25519 key, so nothing new listens on the node), connects to the streamer's loopback `GET /ws/media`, and the portal and node pass messages through untouched. Text messages are the control lines of the WebTransport control stream; binary messages are `cha-stream/1` datagrams (the 16-byte header, no FEC, fragments of at most 64 KiB). The browser decodes them with the same WebCodecs path as WebTransport. PyroWave is not offered over it.
  - **Guests get ICE servers** (`GET /api/shares/{token}/ice`), so a guest who can reach the node directly or through the owner's TURN still uses WebRTC. An internet guest's page tries WebRTC first, then WebSocket.
  - **Rate control on TCP** reads the streamer's own send queue instead of QUIC's: frames that would overflow it are dropped and the stream resyncs with a recovery or key frame, as WebTransport does, and the encoder is held while the queue is deep.
- **Consequences:**
  - The standing rule "no cha.sh-hosted relays" holds: the tunnel is the owner's choice and runs on their Cloudflare account, or anonymously on Cloudflare's. But media of an internet link passes through Cloudflare, which terminates TLS: Cloudflare can see it. The Share dialog says so.
  - WebSocket media is TCP end to end with three hops (Cloudflare, the portal, the node), so a lost packet stalls everything behind it and latency is higher than WebRTC. It is for watching and co-op with a friend, not the owner's own competitive play.
  - Every internet stream crosses the portal twice (in from the node, out to Cloudflare). A portal on a small box carries the guests' bitrate.
  - Nodes and streamers that predate this answer the relay request with an error; the guest's page says the node needs updating.
